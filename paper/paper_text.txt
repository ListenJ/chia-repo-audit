Does Agentic Architecture Discovery Reproduce?
An Audit Gate That Blocked Its Own Headline Result

Abstract
Agents alreadydiscover and exploit loopholes in microar-
chitectural simulators[1], so we measure evaluation va-
lidity rather than design discovery. We release a CHIA
audit loop that separates four variance sources an aggre-
gate score hides (repeated-run, generation-seed, prompt
and trace) and gates publication on both a pre-registered
threshold and a double-blind annotation layer scored by
same-provider models and by a third, architecturally dif-
ferent one. Run in the officialchia-champsimimage, the
loopfailedits own gate: cross-seed CV78.16%against a
5%threshold within one generated series, while repeated-
run CV is0.0000%over 45 runs, so the publish gate reads
Blocked. Two candidates differing only in a struct
name and indentation carry distinct source, design and
binary digests yet bit-identical cycles on all three traces:
the seed moved the artifact, not the design. Ranking is
unstable (top-10.667, Kendallτ= 0.556) and prompt
response reaches249.18%. Annotation givesκ= 0.859
on specification diffs and0.636on real generated C++,

models are correct on all 33 labellable pairs and share

capes. Auditingthatκfoundthedefectinsideit: ourown

self-repairs instead of moving the thresholds.
1 Introduction
Architecture 2.0 pipelines (ArchAgent [1], CHIA [2],
QuArch) let agents propose and evaluate designs with no
humanintheloop. Theriskisnotunclaimed: ArchAgent
reports its pipeline discovered and exploited a loophole
in a popular microarchitectural simulator, inflating re-
ported performance without changing hardware logic [1].
Software-sideevaluationshowsthesamefragility: thetop
three Terminal-Bench 2 submissions were all found guilty
of cheating via harness-level tricks [3], OpenAI stopped
evaluating on SWE-bench Verified and its audit of SWE-
bench Pro found roughly30%of that suite’s tasks bro-
ken [4], and an automated audit of 168 agent benchmarks
flags issues in over25.7%of the evaluatedtasks[5].
What is missing is an instrument, not another warn-
ing: a loop that runs against a real simulator, sepa-
rates the variance sources an aggregate score hides, and
refuses to publish when the separation fails. We con-
tribute that loop plus what it said about us: aBlocked
verdict on our own headline claim (§3.1), evidence that
generation seeds move artifacts without moving designs,
trace-dependent reordering (§3.2) and prompt response
at249.18%(§3.3), a failure mode that text-reading au-
ditors cannot catch (§3.5), and nine defects we found in
our own automation: a vacuous gate axis (§3.3), a gen-
erated ground truth that was wrong while the annota-
tors were right (§3.6), a nullity rule that could not tell
an inert design from an ineffective one (§3.1), a screen-
ing file whose incremental rows cannot be attributed to
the module they name, a measurement attributed to a
source that cannot compile (§3.7), an escalation signal
whose approval score does not separate a local reversible
edit from destroying an instance (§3.11), a harness that
invented the factor levels it was missing (§3.8), a ranking
statistic that printed its own fill value as a measurement
(§3.2), and a commit-pinning gate that the literal string
unknownsatisfies (§3.9).

3 Results
3.1 The gate blocked our own headline
claim
Across 3 designs×3 traces×5 repeats (45 runs,
on a second physical host) the loop reports cross-seed
CV78.16%against the 5% threshold, verdictNon-
Reproducible, publish gateBlocked. Repeated-run
CV is0.0000%: no cell varies across its five runs. The
simulator is deterministic, and determinism is not repro-
ducibility (Fig. 1a).
The replication is exact on measurement and total on
artifact. Cycles, IPC and instruction counts match the
earlier run on all nine cells, yet0 of 9cells share a de-
livered binary digest: a rebuild on another host produces
byte-different binaries while the simulated measurement
stays bit-identical. A binary digest therefore evidences
that a distinct build was delivered, not design identity,
and a replication gate that demanded digest equality
across hosts would reject a faithful reproduction. The
converse failure is present in our own screening data and
is easier to miss: modulenamesare not design identi-
fiers either.gen_fill_only_conservative_s2occurs in
both screening batches under different delivered binaries
(digestsace3ea88...andb83f003a...) and measures
1,132,568in one and1,129,110in the other. Any join
keyed on the module name silently discards one of those
measurements. That is what an earlier version of Fig. 1
did before we keyed it on the digest.
The seed axis explains the gap.s1ands3differ only
in the struct name and two lines of continuation inden-
tation, yet each carries a different source, design and
binary digest while all three traces return bit-identical
cycles.s2keeps a fill history, is 10.6% faster than the
no-op reference on BFSCC, and reproduces the no-op
cycle count exactly on imagick. The seed moved the
artifact and not the design, and nullity is a per-trace
property: our evidence checker used to report a de-
sign that is inert on one workload under the same flag
as one that is inert everywhere, so it now separates
null_on_every_measured_trace(a null design) from a
note recording trace-dependent inertness, a normal result
and not a gate failure.
That78.16%is scoped to one generated series, and a
second series says why. A replication of the same prompt
at seeds{2,3}drawn from adifferentcandidate directory
measures cross-seed CV0.0000%: those two seeds repro-
duce each other bit-exactly on all three traces. The two
directories hold different programs under the same mod-
ule name:gen_fill_only_conservative_s2is 1,210
source characters in one and 1,107 in the other, and its
BFSCC cycle count is 6,420,195 versus 95,624,440. The
15×gap between the two grids is an input difference, not
a measurement disagreement, and the tworaw.jsonfiles
record distinctcandidate_sha256values that are what
made this checkable. A seed label is not a design iden-
tifier across series, and the seed effect reported here is a
property of one series rather than of agentic generation
in general.

3.4 Independent annotators, and where
they agree and are wrong
On the 10 specification cases the two models give Cohen’s
κ=0.859(0.900 agreement, verdict accuracy 10/10 for
both, error-class exactness 7/10 and 8/10). Widening
to pairs of the nine factorial candidates, built from real
generatedC++ratherthandictionarydiffs, initiallygave
κ= 0.7235over 36 pairs withκ=1.0on the verdict axis.
§3.6 shows why that number cannot be quoted: the gold
labels for that set were themselves generated, and three
of its pairs were mislabeled by our own structural rule.
After relabeling,33 of 36pairs carry a derivable verdict,
both annotators are correct onall 33(verdict accuracy
1.0each), they never disagree (κ= 1.0on verdict), and
combined-labelκfalls to0.636. That is nowbelowour
own 0.7 gate too, so both external sets are red, not just
the measured one: the pre-correction 0.7235 was the only
value that passed, and it passed on labels that had to
be withdrawn. On thesixmeasurement-grounded cases
(threerealnullityescapes, addedtotheoriginalfourafter
we found the escape case had cited a source that cannot
compile) the two models do not disagree (κ= 1.0on
verdict, 0 disagreements), and each is right on only 3
of 6 (verdict accuracy0.5); combined-labelκis0.455.
The three cases they miss together areexactlythe three
escapes, and no escape is missed by only one of them.
One caveat we cannot resolve:all 33 labellable gold
labels in that set arenot_equivalent: no pair of gen-
erated candidates was ever labelled equivalent. A rater
that always answersnot_equivalentwould also reach
κ= 1.0on the verdict axis, and ourκhelper returns
exactly 1.0 by convention whenever the marginals are
degenerate. The verdict-only row is thereforenotevi-
dence of reliability on this set; the combined-label 0.636
is the statistic that means something. On the specifica-
tion and measured sets, where both classes occur, verdict
κis non-degenerate.
Read with §3.5, the high verdict agreement is still the
warning, not the reassurance:κmeasures correlation be-
tween annotators, not correctness against measurement,
andκ= 1.0is what a shared blind spot looks like from
the inside. It is also what a sharedcorrectreading looks
like when the ground truth is the defective party. Agree-
ment statistics cannot tell those two apart, which is the
point of §3.6.

missed

Two of the 13 distinct designs in that screening batch

They agree with each other and disagree with the simu-
lator, and this is no longer a single observation. Three of
the six measurement-grounded cases are nullity escapes,
drawn from two screening batches and three distinct de-
livered binaries, and both raters missall threein the
samedirectionwhilemissingnoneofthethreenon-escape
cases. That is a systematic blind spot, not independent
noise, though the denominator is still six and we do not
claim more than that. This result was re-obtained after
we found and fixed an attribution error in the case it-

does not compile against the pinned image, while the
measurement came from a second file in another candi-

E0–E4 also has no slot for “claims to work, measurably
does nothing”: it audits pairwise specification difference,

catches it is the nullity check, not the taxonomy. Reading
a candidate is a poor proxy for running it, and more tex-

4 What this should change
Each rule below is stated as a check an author can run,
because every one of them was violated by us and caught
by reading primary evidence rather than by a green gate.
(1) Report factor levels next to every variance
statistic.A spread computed over fewer than two lev-
els of its own axis must be emitted asunexercised, never
as a value that can satisfy a threshold.(2) T reat la-
bels as generated artifacts.Where a verdict can be
grounded in measurement, ground it; where it cannot,
abstain rather than infer, and fuzz structural labelers
against non-design syntax such as preprocessor lines and
comments.(3) Key identity on the delivered di-
gest, and report the label marginal with every
agreement statistic.κwithout the class distribution
isuninterpretable, andκ= 1.0onasingle-classsetmeans
nothing. Count distinct digests behind the factor labels
too: a level whose design is another level’s design mea-
sures nothing, however consistent its own bookkeeping is.
(4) Do not let a learned score decide what needs
human approvalunless it is shown to separate the safe
action from the destructive one; a rule that escalates ev-
erything automates nothing.(5) Make a nullity check
a gate, not a diagnostic.“Compiles, runs, produces
numbers” passed three designs that do nothing; any ver-
ifier weaker than a comparison against a no-op reference
will pass them too. Two designs in the artifact equal
that reference exactly on all three traces (§3.8), so the
branch is now exercised by a real control rather than a
synthetic one.(6) Record a source digest beside
every measurement.A delivered-binary digest says a
build happened, not which source produced it; a module
name says even less. Our screening rows carried both and
still let a cycle count be attributed to a file that cannot
compile, because the one thing missing was the hash of
the input.(7) F ail closed when a factor level has
no artifact.A harness that substitutes a nearby can-
didate to keep a grid rectangular destroys the axis it is
reporting, and destroys it in both directions: false alarms
from comparing two designs under one axis’s name, and
false passes from measuring one design twice. Refusing
the cell costs one run; the substitution costs the axis, and
leaves no trace in the labels.

6 Conclusion
We released an audit loop that separates four variance
sources, gates publication on measurementandannota-
tion, and keeps its evidence. Against the official CHIA
ChampSim image it blocked its own reproducibility claim
at78.16%cross-seed CV, showed seeds moving artifacts
without moving designs, showed trace choice reordering
candidates (τ= 0.556) and prompt response at249.18%,
and showed two same-provider language-model auditors
agreeing and both wrong on a measured simulator es-
cape, a result that survived re-annotation after we found
the case had cited a source that cannot compile. It
also caught nine silent defects in our own automation:
a gate that passed an unexercised axis, a labeler that
read#includebrackets as comparison operators, a nul-
lityrulethatcouldnottellaninertdesignfromanineffec-
tive one, a screening file whose incremental rows cannot
be attributed to the module they name, a measurement
attributed to a file that cannot produce it, an escalation
rule built on an approval score we then measured not to
separate safe from destructive, a harness that filled an
absent factor level with some other design and stamped
the label on it, a ranking statistic that printed its own
fill value as an instability measurement, and a commit
pin that the stringunknownsatisfied. The instrument,
the negative verdicts, the seven protocol rules we would
ask of any such loop (§4) and the self-reports are the
contribution.
AI assistance in this work
AI coding and writing tools assisted with implementa-
tion, experiment plumbing, and the drafting and editing
of this paper. An AI agent ran the audit loop, wrote and
revised the scripts and the evidence pipeline, and drafted
this text under the author’s direction; the author selected
the claims, checked them against the primary artifacts,
and is responsible for all code, measurements, citations,
claims and the final content here. The evaluation layer is
machine-generated as well: two generative raters, a gen-
erative labeler that we then showed to be wrong while
the raters were right (§3.6), a local discriminative head
used both as a third annotation substrate and as a deci-
siongate(§3.10, §3.11), andnineself-auditsperformedby
the same agent that produced the code it was reviewing.
We state this because the standard this paper applies to
artifacts is the one it must apply to itself: a generated
object counts as evidence only with its producer, its ver-
sion and its failure modes attached. Applied here, that
means the negative verdicts above were produced by a
process that was not independently audited, the reposi-
tory is the thing to check, and this prose is a summary
of it rather than a substitute for it.
